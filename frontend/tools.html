Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the BMI calculator?

BMI is a useful general indicator but doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, or distribution of fat. Athletes with high muscle mass may have a high BMI but low body fat. It's best used as a screening tool rather than a diagnostic measure.

What formula is used for calorie calculation?

We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is considered one of the most accurate formulas for estimating basal metabolic rate (BMR). This is then multiplied by an activity factor to determine your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

Should I eat my maintenance calories to lose weight?

No, to lose weight you need to create a calorie deficit. A safe deficit is typically 300-500 calories below your maintenance level. This should result in a weight loss of about 0.5-1 kg per week. For weight gain, you would eat 300-500 calories above maintenance.

How often should I recalculate my calorie needs?

It's a good idea to recalculate your calorie needs every 4-6 weeks, or whenever your weight changes significantly (5+ kg), your activity level changes substantially, or you hit a plateau in your progress.
